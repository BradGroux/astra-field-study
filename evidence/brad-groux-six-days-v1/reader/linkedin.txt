I've been using GPT-6 Astra with the workflow I developed around GPT-5.6: documented task scope, repository context, standard operating procedures, and verification before delivery. In my testing, I've had to repeat instructions about testing, point back to a supplied writing guide, and ask for requested work missing from a completion handoff.

I reviewed six days of retained Astra records to understand how much activity and corrective steering appeared in my own usage. The records contained 25,254 responses across 557 turns. A separate review of 538 substantive typed and voice contributions identified 147 containing corrective steering, grouped into 78 episodes. Thirty-one episodes contained repeated corrections.

The operating method was already documented in my earlier articles. Eric Provencher from OpenAI's Codex DX team described closely aligned practices in his July guidance for GPT-5.6, including distinct assignments and preserving constraints when delegating:
https://x.com/pvncher/status/2080707291603407077

My established method hasn't carried over reliably in my Astra testing. I suspect shortcuts toward finishing can displace requirements, and requirements can get lost during delegation. Both are entirely untested hypotheses from roughly four or five days of personal experience. The review doesn't establish either cause or a matched performance difference between the models. A deployment runbook also contained outdated instructions.

The counts describe my reviewed conversations, with multiple corrections sometimes addressing one problem. They aren't a model failure rate or a satisfaction score. I've published the methods and reviewed aggregates in Astra Field Study, alongside an interactive explorer and an MIT contribution kit. Raw conversations stay private; contributors review their own aggregate before submitting it:
https://github.com/BradGroux/astra-field-study

This is what I currently think, and I want to know more. If your established workflow transferred cleanly to Astra, I'd like to understand what you used and how you verified the result. Successful examples and experiences that contradict mine are welcome through the repo or by reaching out.
